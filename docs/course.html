Week 1 · Foundations and prediction

Choose one activity to connect a weighted sum or output choice with this week’s prediction notebook.

  • 0.0 · Neuron Explorer

    Trace a weighted sum and ReLU. Change one weight and explain its effect on the output.

    Connect it: Connect the result to one forward pass in the HAR notebook.

  • 6.2.3 · Sigmoid vs Softmax

    Compare independent outputs with mutually exclusive classes. Explain why HAR uses a competing class distribution.

    Connect it: Use the comparison to explain your output-layer choice; this is practice, not a D2L quiz.

Week 2 · Backpropagation and optimization

Choose one activity before checking your implementation or training update.

  • 6.5.2 · Backprop Table

    Follow one chain of local derivatives and explain the sign of the input gradient.

    Connect it: Compare the reasoning with your Assignment 1 finite-difference check.

  • 8.3 · Momentum SGD

    Compare one supplied SGD update with a momentum update. Explain what the velocity remembers.

    Connect it: The supplied gradients illustrate update arithmetic; they are not a benchmark of optimizer quality.

Week 3 · Regularization and validation

Choose one activity to explain your existing baseline/regularization comparison.

  • 7.1 · Regularization Race

    Compare the effect of L1 and L2 on one weight and its update.

    Connect it: Connect one change to the regularization mechanism in Assignment 2; do not add a new required model run.

  • 7.8 · Early Stopping

    Move the stopping epoch and distinguish choosing a checkpoint from fitting its parameters.

    Connect it: The 1,000-example split is a toy exercise. Keep the course’s fixed participant-separated HAR split; do not re-split it.

Week 4 · Convolutional networks

Choose one activity to explain a convolution or a pooling output shape.

  • 9.1 · Convolution

    Work through one filter position and predict the next output.

    Connect it: The example is two-dimensional; relate the same shared-filter idea to the one-dimensional HAR signal candidate.

  • 9.3 · Adaptive Pooling

    Change the input length and compare fixed-window pooling with a fixed output size.

    Connect it: Explain what remains fixed before the dense classifier. This is optional architecture practice.

Week 5 · Recurrence and attention

Choose one activity to explain where sequence order enters a model.

  • 10.1.2 · RNN Forward

    Trace one recurrent update, then compare the same inputs in a different order.

    Connect it: Relate the hidden-state trace to time-ordered HAR windows.

  • 10.TF · Transformer Positional Encoding

    Compare reordered tokens with and without position information.

    Connect it: Use the permutation comparison to explain why a signal-attention candidate needs time-position information.

Week 6 · Evaluation and interpretation

Choose one activity to support an error analysis you already need for the project.

  • 11.1 · Choosing the Right Metric

    Compare accuracy and recall as the decision threshold changes.

    Connect it: All clinical counts are a teaching scenario, not clinical evidence. Transfer the metric trade-off to a HAR confusion matrix.

  • 11.GA · Gradient Attribution

    Compare a gradient with a feature-removal effect in one supplied toy example.

    Connect it: Explain why an attribution score depends on the method and reference. It is not proof of a causal explanation.

Week 7 · Experiment synthesis

Reuse your existing curves and results; no new training run is needed.

  • 11.3 · Learning Curves

    Use a training/validation pattern to distinguish poor fit from a generalization gap.

    Connect it: Apply one diagnosis to your baseline/candidate comparison and state a limitation. Do not collect new data or exceed the project run budget.
